It’s never about them. It’s never about the other person.
It’s about how we feel towards ourselves in their presence.
In limerence, we try to work up the courage to say, “I love you.”
When what we mean to say is, “I love myself when I’m with you.”

Humans are strange creatures;
Their hearts possess the unique ability to turn scars into flowers,
flowers which only bloom in the light of another. In isolation,
We see only our wounds and scars. In limerence,
The presence of another lets us see our flowers bloom
And the colors make us feel worthy of our own love.

To love oneself is the precursor to loving another.
But before we get there, the past echoes—
All good things must come to an end.

In limerence, fear replaces love.
Not fear of rejection or being alone or being unloved.
But fear of falling back into the darkness of ourselves.
Fear tells us that we will never see light again,
So we must cling to it now.

But love is different.

Love is bonded individuals burning their flowers
So the other can find their way through the darkness.
And in this way they perpetually navigate, interdependent,
both the light and the darkness, separate yet together.

In limerence, we mean, “I love myself when I’m with you.”
In love, we mean, “I love myself, so I can love you, so you can love yourself, so you can love me, so I can love...”
